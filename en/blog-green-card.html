Green card 2026: when you need it, what it costs and how to get one

Updated: July 2026

The most common mix-up on summer trips: people assume a green card is needed almost everywhere, when in fact it is mandatory for very few of the countries on Gasolina's routes. Here is exactly where it is required, where it is not, and how to get one in ten minutes.

Where it is genuinely required (and where it is not)

⚠️ Required

  • North Macedonia
  • Albania

✓ Not required

  • Croatia, Slovenia
  • Bosnia and Herzegovina
  • Montenegro
  • Hungary, Bulgaria, Romania
  • Greece

The reason: since 2012 Serbia has been part of the Multilateral Agreement — the number plate and its registration sticker are accepted as proof of insurance in every signatory country, with no separate international card. Montenegro and Bosnia are additionally covered by a bilateral agreement. North Macedonia and Albania are outside that system, which is why their border police still ask for the card.

The classic mistake on the way to Greece. Greece itself does not require a green card — but almost everyone drives there through North Macedonia, where it is mandatory. Route yourself through Bulgaria instead and you will not need a green card anywhere on the trip.

Price and validity

Around 2,000 dinars at the AMSS or the insurer that issued your third-party liability policy; up to 3,000 dinars through an intermediary or a different insurance company. The price is fixed — it does not depend on how many days you travel.

Validity does not run from the day of issue — the card is valid exactly until your third-party policy, and therefore your vehicle registration, expires. If your registration runs out in 20 days, a card issued today covers those 20 days, not a year. That is why it makes most sense to pick one up together with your registration rather than separately before each trip.

How to get one

  • Go to the insurer that issued your third-party policy (or to the AMSS)
  • Bring: your third-party insurance policy and the vehicle registration document
  • You get the card on the spot — the whole thing takes a few minutes
  • Some insurers and the AMSS also issue it online (PDF by e-mail, same price)

If you forget it or do not have one

Some border crossings have an insurance branch where you can buy a temporary border policy with a green card on the spot — but it is dearer (around €20) and covers only 7 days, against roughly €17 (2,000 RSD) for a card bought in advance that runs until your registration expires. Treat this as a fallback, not as the plan.

Common questions

Which countries require a green card on Serbian plates?

In practice only North Macedonia and Albania. Croatia, Bosnia, Montenegro, Slovenia, Hungary, Bulgaria, Romania and Greece do not require one.

How much does a green card cost?

Around 2,000 dinars at the AMSS or your own insurer, up to 3,000 through an intermediary. A fixed price, regardless of trip length.

How long is a green card valid?

Until your third-party policy and vehicle registration expire, not for a fixed period from the day of issue.

Sources: AMSS, DDOR, Association of Serbian Insurers. Rules can change — check with your insurer before you travel.
