About the project

Gasolina is an independent site about driving through Serbia and the surrounding region: waiting times and live cameras at border crossings, fuel prices by country, and a calculator that works out where it pays to fill up. It is built by one person, in Serbia, with no newsroom and no sponsors.

Last updated: 12 August 2026.

Why it exists

Before a trip people ask the same two things: how bad is the border, and where should I buy fuel. The answers exist, but scattered — the wait on one institution's site, the camera on another's, fuel prices somewhere else again, and the neighbouring countries each in their own language. Gasolina puts that in one place and, more importantly, says where every figure came from.

For Debeli Brijeg and Dojran we show the camera but no waiting time — these are not Serbian borders and none of the sources we follow publishes an estimate for them. The site says so, instead of inventing a number.

Alongside that: diesel, petrol and LPG prices in Serbia and neighbouring countries, price history since 2024, a per-route cost calculator with a suggestion of where to fill up, and three guides (the Bulgarian vignette, the Green Card, documents for Greece).

The rule everything rests on

The site must not claim what it does not know. Better to write "no reliable estimate" than to put up a figure that merely looks convincing. Someone who sets off once on a false number does not come back — and they are right not to.

That reads like a slogan until it starts costing something. A few things that were taken off the site because of it, even though they looked harmless:

  • Fallback figures. The site carried example values in case the data failed to load. They worked quietly and convincingly. They are gone: when data does not arrive, the site now says so.
  • A zero from traffic measurement treated as proof of calm. Measurement only sees moving vehicles, so a queue standing at passport control does not exist for it. Until August 2026 such a zero would erase a four-hour driver report. Not any more — the methodology explains why.
  • "Live" with nothing behind it. The page said "live" even when the figures were an hour and a half old. Now every figure carries its age, and past an hour the word "live" is withdrawn.
  • One number when sources disagree. The larger one used to win. Now, when two estimates differ by more than a factor of three, the site shows both and says there is no verdict.

Where coverage ends

This is the list of what the site does not do, written down here so it does not have to be discovered the hard way:

  • Not every crossing is covered. Thirteen are, the rest are not. Expanding waits on evidence that a new crossing actually has a source publishing a real value rather than a default one.
  • Traffic measurement does not exist for every direction. Of 26 directions, ten have no corridor — among them Spiljani and Sremska Raca in both directions.
  • We do not forecast congestion. The site says what the sources report now, not how long the wait will be in three hours.
  • Reports from our own form are rare. Over forty days, exactly one usable report arrived. Driver reports therefore rely mostly on BorderAlarm, and the form stands for the cases where something is obviously wrong.
  • Freight traffic is tracked less well than passenger traffic. Sources for lorries are sparser and less consistent.

Money and independence

The project currently earns nothing. If advertising ever appears on the site it will be clearly marked, and it will not influence which crossings are tracked or which figure is shown — that decision is made in a single file that knows nothing about advertising, and every change to it passes automated checks before going live.

Corrections and contact

If a figure does not match what is happening on the ground, that is valuable information and I would like to hear it: kontakt@gasolina.rs. The time, the crossing and the direction help most — with those the exact record can be found in the archive and the source that got it wrong identified. Several changes to this site started exactly that way.
