Documents for driving into Greece 2026

Updated: July 2026

A short, concrete list of what you actually need to drive from Serbia to Greece — whether you go through North Macedonia or Bulgaria — plus two things that changed this year and are worth knowing before you set off.

The basic list

  • Biometric passport — must be valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned departure from Greece. An ID card is NOT valid for Greece (Schengen), even if you fly.
  • Driving licence and vehicle registration document — a given, as on any trip.
  • Green card — only if you travel through North Macedonia or Albania (not needed for Greece itself, nor for the route via Bulgaria). Details in the separate guide.
  • International authorisation (the yellow card) — only if you drive someone else's vehicle rather than your own. 1,500 RSD, issued by the AMSS with the owner present.
  • European accident statement — the standard accident report form; worth keeping in the car.
  • Fire extinguisher — mandatory in Greece (minimum S2, 2 kg).
Children travelling without both parents. If a child under 16 travels with only one parent, with grandparents or in an organised group, you need a notarised consent form from the absent parent (roughly 1,500–2,500 RSD per certification in Belgrade, about ten minutes). This is the one people most often forget until they are standing at the border.

Two changes for 2026 worth knowing

The EES system is suspended over the summer. The new EU biometric system (fingerprints plus a photograph at the border) has formally been in place since April 2026, but in Greece it is suspended from 1 June to 30 August because of summer congestion at the border. In practice: over the summer of 2026, do not expect that procedure at Evzoni or the other crossings.
ETIAS is not mandatory yet. The new online travel authorisation (similar to the American ESTA) is announced for Serbian citizens in the last quarter of 2026, but no start date has been confirmed. It is unlikely to apply for the 2026 summer season — check the official announcements before you travel if you are going later in the year.

The TAG device — faster through Greek toll plazas

Greek toll plazas (Egnatia Odos, towards Halkidiki and Thessaloniki) have dedicated lanes for the electronic TAG device. The device itself is free, but you pay a minimum credit of €15 when you collect it (topped up when it drops to €2.50, through the MyEgnatiaPass app or at the plaza). In July and August the difference between the TAG lane and the ordinary lanes can be 20–30 minutes per plaza.

Currently important. Postal delivery of the TAG device to addresses in Serbia is temporarily suspended. Instead of ordering it in advance, the device is now collected in person at the first toll plaza near Evzoni (weekdays 09:00–17:00) or at other sales points. Do not count on it arriving by post before your trip.

Bonus: if you already have a Serbian TAG device (from Pošta Srbije), it has also worked at North Macedonian toll plazas since 2023 — you do not need a separate device just to transit North Macedonia.

Practical details people miss

  • The customs limit on cigarettes from Serbia is 40 (two packs) — far lower than people expect from Croatia (200 there). Above that you face a fine and confiscation.
  • Spare glasses — if your driving licence says you drive with glasses, Greece requires you to carry a spare pair in the car. This rule applies there and nowhere else on the route.
  • Travel health insurance is not formally mandatory, but it is strongly recommended — Serbia is not in the EU and you have no EHIC card, so without private cover you pay the full price for any medical treatment.

Common questions

Do I need a passport for Greece or is an ID card enough?

You need a biometric passport. An ID card is not valid for the Schengen area, only for a few neighbouring non-EU countries.

Do I need a green card for Greece?

Not for Greece itself, but almost everyone drives through North Macedonia, where it is mandatory. Via Bulgaria you need one nowhere on the route.

Is the EES system in force in the summer of 2026?

No — it is suspended in Greece from 1 June to 30 August 2026 because of summer congestion.

How do I get a Greek toll TAG device?

The device is free with a minimum credit of €15. Postal delivery to Serbia is currently suspended, so it is collected in person at the first toll plaza near Evzoni (weekdays 09:00–17:00). A Serbian TAG from Pošta Srbije also works at North Macedonian plazas.

Sources: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Serbia, YUTA, grckainfo.com. The EU rules (EES/ETIAS) are still being rolled out and may change — check the official announcements before you travel.
